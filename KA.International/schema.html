Q2: What is the difference between a hospital curtain and a normal home curtain? A: Hospital curtains are made from fabrics intended for frequent washing and are usually flame-retardant, and they often have a mesh panel at the top for air and light flow. Home curtains are mainly decorative and are not designed for repeated laundering under infection-control routines. Q3: What is a hospital cubicle track and why is it fixed to the ceiling? A: A cubicle track is a lightweight rail, usually made of aluminium, that holds curtain carriers so the curtain slides smoothly around a bed. It is fixed to or suspended from the ceiling so that the floor stays clear for beds, trolleys and equipment. Q4: What is the difference between an IV hanger and an IV pole? A: An IV hanger is an overhead fitting with hooks for infusion bags, mounted on the ceiling or on a track. An IV pole is a free-standing, wheeled stand that stands on the floor. Overhead hangers keep the floor area free, while poles can be moved anywhere in the room. Q5: Which products help control dust and insects at hospital entrances? A: Air curtains and PVC strip curtains are the two common options. An air curtain blows a fast stream of air across the opening, while a PVC strip curtain is a physical barrier made of overlapping flexible strips. Neither replaces proper door and ventilation design. Q6: What should be checked before choosing ward curtains, tracks or partitions? A: Check the room dimensions, ceiling height and ceiling type, the fabric or material requirements for fire safety and washing, the expected traffic and cleaning method, and any fire-safety or accreditation requirements of the facility. A: Hospital curtains, also called cubicle or privacy curtains, divide a bed or treatment space from the rest of the room to give patients privacy. They are used in wards, ICUs, emergency areas, dialysis units, examination rooms and clinics. Q2: How often should hospital curtains be washed or changed? A: There is no single universal rule, so the facility's infection-control policy decides. Common practice is to change curtains when they are visibly soiled, after a patient on isolation precautions is discharged, and at routine intervals such as every one to three months or quarterly, depending on the risk level of the area. Q3: What fabric is best for hospital curtains? A: Polyester and polyester-blend fabrics are widely used because they are durable, wash well and can be made flame-retardant. Inherently flame-retardant yarns keep their protection after repeated washing, while chemically treated fabrics may lose protection over time, so the fabric's test or certification details should be checked. Q4: How do I decide the size of a hospital curtain? A: Measure the length of the track run and the drop from the track to the floor. The finished curtain is made wider than the track so that it hangs in folds, and it usually stops a short distance above the floor to ease cleaning and airflow. The exact fullness and floor gap should be confirmed before ordering. Q5: Why do hospital curtains have a mesh panel at the top? A: The mesh or net panel allows air, light and, where fitted, fire-sprinkler water to pass through, while the solid fabric below gives privacy. It also lets staff see the general condition of the space above the curtain line. Q6: Are disposable curtains better than reusable curtains? A: Disposable curtains are replaced instead of laundered, so they are often chosen for isolation rooms or high-risk areas. Reusable curtains are washed and rehung, which can cost less over time but needs a laundering and tracking routine. The right choice depends on the area, budget and infection-control policy. A: A hospital cubicle track is a rail fixed to the ceiling that carries curtain hooks or carriers, allowing a privacy curtain to glide smoothly around a bed. It can be straight, L-shaped or U-shaped to match the bed layout. Q2: What is the difference between surface-mounted and suspended cubicle tracks? A: A surface-mounted track is fixed directly to the ceiling, while a suspended track hangs from rods or hangers. Suspended mounting is used when the ceiling is high or cannot take a direct fixing; some specifications recommend hangers when ceilings are higher than about 2.7 metres, to keep curtain length practical. Q3: What material is used for cubicle tracks? A: Extruded aluminium is the most common material because it is light, resists rust and can be bent into curves. It is usually finished in white powder coating or silver anodising. Q4: Can a cubicle track be curved, and how tight can the bend be? A: Yes, aluminium tracks can be bent to form corners and U-shapes. Some specifications ask for a minimum bend radius of around 450 mm, because tighter bends can make carriers stick, so the manufacturer's limit should be followed. Q5: How is a cubicle track fixed to a false ceiling? A: A track is not normally loaded onto the false-ceiling tile. It is fixed to the structural slab with suspension rods, or clipped to the ceiling grid where the grid is rated for the load. The fixing method should be checked with the ceiling installer. A: As a general guide, about 2 mm is used for personnel doors at normal temperature, about 3 mm for cold rooms and mixed traffic, and about 4 mm for heavy traffic such as forklifts. Thicker strips hang flatter but need more force to push through. Q2: How much should PVC strips overlap? A: Strips normally overlap by about 30 to 50 percent. A 50 percent overlap is commonly used for cold rooms and areas where temperature control matters, while a lower overlap lets people and trolleys pass more easily. Q3: How do I measure a doorway for a PVC strip curtain? A: Measure the clear width of the opening and the height from the mounting point to the floor. Because of the overlap, the total strip width ordered is usually 30 to 50 percent more than the opening width, and the strips should stop just above the floor. Q4: What is the difference between standard and polar-grade PVC? A: Standard PVC becomes stiff and can crack at very low temperatures. Polar or freezer-grade PVC stays flexible in cold conditions and is the type normally specified for cold rooms and freezers. Q5: How long do PVC strip curtains last? A: Life depends on traffic and environment. Supplier guidance suggests about 4 to 6 years for light pedestrian use and 1 to 2 years where forklifts hit the strips regularly, with cold-room use falling in between. A: Sheer or sunscreen fabrics reduce glare while keeping a view outside. Light-filtering fabrics let in soft light and give privacy. For an outside mount, measure the area to be covered and add overlap on each side. Q5: How do I clean a roller blind? A: Dust it regularly with a soft brush or dry cloth, and wipe marks with a slightly damp cloth and mild soap. Soaking the fabric or pulling hard on the tube should be avoided. Laminated glass holds together when cracked and gives better sound reduction. Frosted or film-covered glass adds privacy. The choice depends on safety, privacy and acoustic needs. Q5: How do aluminium partitions compare with brick or gypsum walls? A: Aluminium partitions are lighter, quicker to install and easier to relocate. Sound insulation and fire resistance depend on the specific system, glass and seals used, so those ratings should be confirmed rather than assumed.

A: Hospital curtains are made from fabrics intended for frequent washing and are usually flame-retardant, and they often have a mesh panel at the top for air and light flow. Home curtains are mainly decorative and are not designed for repeated laundering under infection-control routines. Q3: What is a hospital cubicle track and why is it fixed to the ceiling? A: A cubicle track is a lightweight rail, usually made of aluminium, that holds curtain carriers so the curtain slides smoothly around a bed. It is fixed to or suspended from the ceiling so that the floor stays clear for beds, trolleys and equipment. Q4: What is the difference between an IV hanger and an IV pole? A: An IV hanger is an overhead fitting with hooks for infusion bags, mounted on the ceiling or on a track. An IV pole is a free-standing, wheeled stand that stands on the floor. Overhead hangers keep the floor area free, while poles can be moved anywhere in the room. Q5: Which products help control dust and insects at hospital entrances? A: Air curtains and PVC strip curtains are the two common options. An air curtain blows a fast stream of air across the opening, while a PVC strip curtain is a physical barrier made of overlapping flexible strips. Neither replaces proper door and ventilation design. A: An air curtain is a fan unit mounted above an opening that blows a fast, narrow stream of air downward across the doorway. This moving air forms an invisible barrier that reduces the exchange of air, dust and insects between the two sides of the opening. Q2: How do I choose the right size of air curtain? A: The unit should be at least as wide as the opening, and wide openings can be covered with two or more units placed end to end. The air stream must also reach the floor with enough speed, so the door height must fall within the unit’s rated mounting height. Q3: Can an air curtain replace a door? A: No. An air curtain reduces air exchange across an open doorway but does not stop people, wind or pressure differences in the way a closed door does. It works best as an addition to a door or opening that is frequently used. Q4: Where should an air curtain be installed? A: It is usually mounted horizontally above the door on the inside, with a clear path for the air to travel from top to floor. Obstructions such as signs, lights or door hardware in the air path reduce performance. Q5: Should I choose a heated or unheated air curtain? A: Unheated (ambient) units are used to keep out insects and dust in most warm-climate buildings. Heated units are used mainly where a significant temperature difference has to be managed, such as in cold climates. Q6: Do air curtains stop flying insects? A: They can greatly reduce the number of flying insects entering an opening when the unit covers the full width and the air reaches the floor. A: There is no single universal rule, so the facility's infection-control policy decides. Common practice is to change curtains when they are visibly soiled, after a patient on isolation precautions is discharged, and at routine intervals such as every one to three months or quarterly, depending on the risk level of the area. Q3: What fabric is best for hospital curtains? A: Polyester and polyester-blend fabrics are widely used because they are durable, wash well and can be made flame-retardant. Inherently flame-retardant yarns keep their protection after repeated washing, while chemically treated fabrics may lose protection over time, so the fabric's test or certification details should be checked. Q4: How do I decide the size of a hospital curtain? A: Measure the length of the track run and the drop from the track to the floor. The finished curtain is made wider than the track so that it hangs in folds, and it usually stops a short distance above the floor to ease cleaning and airflow. The exact fullness and floor gap should be confirmed before ordering. Q5: Why do hospital curtains have a mesh panel at the top? A: The mesh or net panel allows air, light and, where fitted, fire-sprinkler water to pass through, while the solid fabric below gives privacy. It also lets staff see the general condition of the space above the curtain line. Q6: Are disposable curtains better than reusable curtains? A: Disposable curtains are replaced instead of laundered, so they are often chosen for isolation rooms or high-risk areas. Reusable curtains are washed and rehung, which can cost less over time but needs a laundering and tracking routine. The right choice depends on the area, budget and infection-control policy. A: A hospital cubicle track is a rail fixed to the ceiling that carries curtain hooks or carriers, allowing a privacy curtain to glide smoothly around a bed. It can be straight, L-shaped or U-shaped to match the bed layout. Q2: What is the difference between surface-mounted and suspended cubicle tracks? A: A surface-mounted track is fixed directly to the ceiling, while a suspended track hangs from rods or hangers. Suspended mounting is used when the ceiling is high or cannot take a direct fixing; some specifications recommend hangers when ceilings are higher than about 2.7 metres, to keep curtain length practical. Q3: What material is used for cubicle tracks? A: Extruded aluminium is the most common material because it is light, resists rust and can be bent into curves. It is usually finished in white powder coating or silver anodising. Q4: Can a cubicle track be curved, and how tight can the bend be? A: Yes, aluminium tracks can be bent to form corners and U-shapes. Some specifications ask for a minimum bend radius of around 450 mm, because tighter bends can make carriers stick, so the manufacturer's limit should be followed. Q5: How is a cubicle track fixed to a false ceiling? A: A track is not normally loaded onto the false-ceiling tile. It is fixed to the structural slab with suspension rods, or clipped to the ceiling grid where the grid is rated for the load. The fixing method should be checked with the ceiling installer. A: As a general guide, about 2 mm is used for personnel doors at normal temperature, about 3 mm for cold rooms and mixed traffic, and about 4 mm for heavy traffic such as forklifts. Thicker strips hang flatter but need more force to push through. Q2: How much should PVC strips overlap? A: Strips normally overlap by about 30 to 50 percent. A 50 percent overlap is commonly used for cold rooms and areas where temperature control matters, while a lower overlap lets people and trolleys pass more easily. Q3: How do I measure a doorway for a PVC strip curtain? A: Measure the clear width of the opening and the height from the mounting point to the floor. Because of the overlap, the total strip width ordered is usually 30 to 50 percent more than the opening width, and the strips should stop just above the floor. Q4: What is the difference between standard and polar-grade PVC? A: Standard PVC becomes stiff and can crack at very low temperatures. Polar or freezer-grade PVC stays flexible in cold conditions and is the type normally specified for cold rooms and freezers. Q5: How long do PVC strip curtains last? A: Life depends on traffic and environment. Supplier guidance suggests about 4 to 6 years for light pedestrian use and 1 to 2 years where forklifts hit the strips regularly, with cold-room use falling in between.
